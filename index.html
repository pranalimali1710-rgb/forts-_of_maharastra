The Strategic Evolution of Deccan Fortifications

History and heritage of Maharashtra

Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj built over 350 forts in Maharashtra, classified into Giri-durg (hill-forts), Jala-durg (sea-forts), and Bhuikot (land-forts).


These structures served as military bases, administrative centres and strategic hubs, making them the backbone of the Maratha Empire.


The forts of Maharashtra represent the courage, planning, architecture and military strategy of the Maratha period.

Famous Forts of Maharashtra

Geographical, historical and architectural information

1. Raigad Fort – The Sovereign Capital of Swarajya

Geographical Context: Located in the Raigad district, this massive hill fortress stands at an elevation of 820 meters (2,700 feet) above its base within the Sahyadri mountain range. Its steep slopes made it naturally inaccessible to hostile forces.

Detailed History: Originally known as the fortress of Rairi, Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj captured it from the More family of Javli in 1656. Recognizing its strategic importance, he completely restructured the site into the capital of the expanding Maratha Empire. It was here, on June 6, 1674, that Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj was formally crowned, establishing the sovereign state of Hindavi Swarajya.

Architectural Layout: The fort is accessed via the imposing Maha Darwaja, which is flanked by massive defensive bastions. The internal plateau houses the Hirakani Bastion, the Raigad royal court, the Jagadishwar Temple and a sprawling market street designed to handle large-scale trade and business directly from horseback.

2. Rajgad Fort – The Foundation of Empire Building

Geographical Context: Situated roughly 48 kilometres southwest of Pune, Rajgad stands on the Murumbadev mountain, offering a commanding panoramic view of the surrounding Nira and Gunjavane river valleys.

Detailed History: Rajgad serves as the foundational bedrock of the Maratha Empire. Captured by a young Shivaji Maharaj in 1647, it functioned as the primary administrative and military centre for a continuous period of 26 years. The fort witnessed critical historical timelines, including the birth of Chhatrapati Rajaram Maharaj and strategic planning of the empire from Agra.

Architectural Layout: Rajgad is renowned for its advanced defensive layout, split into three fortified plateaus known as Machis:

  • Padmavati Machi
  • Sanjivani Machi
  • Suvela Machi

These plateaus have hidden exit paths called Chor-Darwajas for surprise counter-attacks. At the highest peak sits the Balekilla, the heavily fortified inner sanctuary containing water reservoirs and sacred structures.

3. Sindhudurg Fort – The Sentinel of the Malvan Coast

Geographical Context: Unlike mainland fortresses, Sindhudurg is a marine fort constructed entirely on Kurte Island, a rocky islet in the Arabian Sea right off the coast of Malvan in the Konkan region.

Detailed History: Construction began in November 1664 under the direct supervision of Shivaji Maharaj. The objective was to neutralize the maritime dominance of foreign trading powers, including the English and Dutch, and to curb the naval raids of the Siddis of Janjira. Over three years, thousands of stonemasons completed a fortress that successfully anchored the Maratha maritime defence.

Architectural Layout: The fort's foundational engineering is a historic marvel. Builders melted down and poured massive mounds of lead and iron directly into the sea bedrock to anchor the structure. The perimeter features a 4-kilometre-long zigzag wall reaching heights of up to 30 feet, specifically designed to deflect naval cannon fire. The entrance is masterfully hidden. Inside the complex lie freshwater wells that remain active year-round despite being surrounded by seawater.

4. Pratapgad Fort – The Fortress of Valor

Geographical Context: Built in the Satara district near the popular hill station of Mahabaleshwar, Pratapgad stands at an altitude of approximately 1,080 metres. It sits on a strategic mountain crest dividing the surrounding region from the inland Deccan plateau.

Detailed History: Commissioned by Chhatrapati Shivaji Maharaj to command the rebellious Javli valley, the fort was completed by his prime minister Moropant Pingale in 1656. The fort is famous for the Battle of Pratapgad, fought on November 10, 1659, where Shivaji Maharaj defeated the Adilshahi commander Afzal Khan. This victory established the Marathas as a formidable military power in the region.
